# RegMap Funding Strategy: Grants and Angel Capital

The plan blends non-dilutive money (grants, which you do not give equity for) with a small amount of dilutive angel capital. This keeps founder ownership high while still getting the business off the ground. All amounts and program details below are typical figures and should be confirmed against each program's current solicitation before applying.

## 1. Why a blended strategy

- Grants fund research and productization without taking equity, and an award is a strong external validation signal that de-risks the angel round.
- Angel capital moves faster than grants and funds the commercial work (sales, onboarding, SOC 2) that grants often will not cover.
- Together they extend runway further than either alone, which is exactly what a first-time raise needs.

## 2. Non-dilutive track (grants)

### 2.1 NSF SBIR and STTR (America's Seed Fund)
- **Fit:** strong. This is a deep-technology program for startups commercializing novel research, which is precisely our profile (fine-tuned models, reproducible benchmarks, published-standard research).
- **Phase I** is typically around 275,000 for feasibility work over six to twelve months. **Phase II** can be roughly 1,000,000 to scale it.
- **The STTR angle is a genuine advantage for us.** STTR requires a formal partnership with a research institution. The founder's DePaul University affiliation and existing research make DePaul a natural research partner, which many startups cannot easily arrange. This turns an academic relationship into a funding advantage.
- **Action:** submit a Project Pitch to NSF early (the pitch is short and free), and in parallel scope an STTR with a DePaul faculty collaborator.

### 2.2 Defense and homeland-security SBIR (DoD, Air Force, DHS)
- **Fit:** strong, and often faster and larger than civilian programs. Our operational-technology and industrial-control-systems models and the CMMC compliance angle map directly to defense-supplier and critical-infrastructure needs.
- **Why us:** defense suppliers must satisfy CMMC and related frameworks, which is a crosswalking problem against NIST 800-171 and 800-53. This is a fundable, mission-relevant use case.
- **Action:** monitor DoD SBIR and DHS SBIR topic releases for compliance-automation and critical-infrastructure topics, and respond with the CMMC and OT/ICS story.

### 2.3 State and regional programs (Illinois and Chicago)
- **Fit:** moderate, useful for smaller top-ups and matching funds.
- Illinois and the Chicago ecosystem run technology and small-business support programs, and some states offer SBIR matching grants that add to a federal award.
- The DePaul and Chicago startup community also provides non-dilutive credits (cloud, legal, tooling) that reduce burn.

### 2.4 Cloud and vendor credits
- Startup programs from major cloud providers commonly grant tens of thousands of dollars in infrastructure credits, which directly offsets our cloud line.
- **Action:** apply on incorporation, ideally through an accelerator or investor referral that unlocks larger credit tiers.

## 3. Dilutive track (angel and pre-seed)

- **Instrument:** a SAFE (simple agreement for future equity), the standard, low-cost instrument for pre-seed rounds. It avoids setting a priced valuation too early.
- **Primary ask:** 750,000, with a post-money valuation cap to be negotiated in a range that keeps founder ownership healthy while giving the angel meaningful upside.
- **Alternative:** a 1,100,000 SAFE that funds the full 18 months without depending on grant timing.
- **Who to approach:**
  - Angels with security, compliance, or GRC operating backgrounds, who understand the pain and can open doors to first customers.
  - Pre-seed funds focused on security, developer tools, or applied artificial intelligence.
  - Angel groups and syndicates in the Chicago and Midwest ecosystem, and university-affiliated investor networks through DePaul.
- **What closes the round:** the working platform, the released models, the reproducible benchmarks, a grant award or a strong grant pipeline, and one or two named design partners.

## 4. Accelerators (optional booster)

- A top accelerator provides a small check, a large network, cloud and tool credits, and fundraising momentum.
- This is optional and should be pursued only if it accelerates customer access rather than just diluting further.

## 5. Sequence and timeline

1. **Now:** incorporate, open cloud-credit and vendor-credit applications, and submit the NSF Project Pitch.
2. **Months 0 to 3:** run the angel raise (750,000 SAFE) using the working product and research as proof, and line up the DePaul STTR partnership.
3. **Months 3 to 9:** submit SBIR or STTR Phase I, sign design partners, and begin SOC 2.
4. **Months 6 to 12:** convert design partners to paid, secure the first grant award, and use both to begin the seed conversation.
5. **Around month 12:** open the seed round on the strength of revenue, the grant, and named customers.

## 6. What each dollar unlocks

- The angel 750,000 buys the team and the product build that produce first customers.
- The grant 275,000 to 400,000 funds continued research and productization and validates the technology to future investors.
- Early revenue and credits stretch the runway to the seed milestone.

The message to an angel is simple: your capital is the catalyst that unlocks non-dilutive grant money and first revenue, so a modest check does more here than in a company that has only an idea.
